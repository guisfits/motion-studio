# Principles: why the editorial explainer works

Legend: **[S]** sourced (see `sources.md`) · **[M]** measured in the 28-reel reference studies (`inspirations/`) · **[I]** inference.

## 1. The contract with the viewer

An explainer is a promise: *you have a question (or did not know you had one); I will show you the
evidence; you will leave with one insight you can repeat.* Vox's founding aim was explanatory
journalism, "Understand the news", later "Be curious" [S Posner, Video Consortium]. Lalwani: ideas
start from "what do you want to know about?", and the piece must "over-deliver on context" and have
a "memorability factor" [S Press Gazette].

Three obligations follow:

| Obligation | What it means in the film | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Question first** | The opening sets up a question and convinces the viewer they need the answer before any information arrives | [S JEA guide; Fong black-hole: "I wanted to get people to understand why this is so difficult before they saw the result"] |
| **Evidence, shown** | The argument is carried by things on screen: documents, maps, numbers, archival photos; "the whole point of video is that you're talking about the thing onscreen" | [S Caswell, Film Independent] |
| **One insight that sticks** | Fong hopes each piece "has a moment that might stick in people's minds" rather than "so much information that almost nothing really sticks" | [S Fong, Video Consortium] |

Klein's rule, repeated by Posner: *never underestimate the intelligence of your audience, and never
overestimate the information they have* [S]. It sets both tone (no condescension, no sanitising,
Sendaydiego [S Storybench]) and density (define, then go deep).

## 2. "Show the evidence" is the whole style

- **Visual-first gate.** At Vox a story belonged on video only if it had distinctly visual
  elements; stock footage over narration did not qualify [S The Long Story]. Abram's test: find
  "the visual that helps viewers understand it" and let it guide the story; never fill a gap with
  on-camera talk "just because I didn't have a good visual" [S Video Consortium].
- **Literal correspondence.** Fong aims for "the perfect correspondence between what you're seeing
  in these animations and what you're hearing" because it boosts comprehension and retention [S].
  Measured: in every A-grade reel each noun in the voice gets its object on screen within the same
  sentence; "Numbers. Places. Historical events." = counter, map, poster, one shot each [M Zhylin].
- **Documents are the hero.** Edwards builds each video around a "Rosetta stone", one key
  document that supplies backstory and argument [S The Explainers]. On screen that document is
  pushed into, highlighted and held; it is the proof, not wallpaper.
- **Numbers, one at a time.** One number or name per beat, set huge (381, 1930, 13 TEAMS) [M
  earn.edits]; third-party Vox guides converge on "one number per scene, a bar chart with one
  highlighted bar over a table" [S Korpi, third-party].
- **Recurring visual anchor.** For a topic that is not naturally visual, Vox's art director finds
  a repeating motif (flying words for AI) and builds the film around it [S Sendaydiego].

## 3. Texture as authenticity

The editorial look says "this came from somewhere": a scan, a print, a photograph. Texture is
evidence of provenance, which is why it reads as credible and a flat vector world (Kurzgesagt) does
not belong to this school.

- Never a flat solid background: layer real scans behind the subject, set blacks to dark grey
  rather than pure black, lift whites slightly, add a tint, then a little texture; contrast stays
  subtle [M chrismoran-editorial, stated in the reel].
- Texture must be scaled to the object it sits on; a texture too large reads "off", the same
  texture tiled at the right scale reads tactile [M @chrismoran__ DcBhNhOg5kR].
- Imperfection is the signal: slight blur of halftone dots, ink bleed, off-white paper, "tiny
  imperfections" after a scan + high-pass + levels [S collage guides; M @chrismoran__ DbJSUGZpvzL].
- Johnny Harris's graphics and photos typically layer textured overlays, film burns, light leaks
  and noise/grain [S Motion Array].
- Vox changes art direction per topic (construction paper for a teaching story, 1960s protest
  poster language for Fred Hampton) but always a made-by-hand material [S Sendaydiego].

## 4. Restraint and hierarchy

Measured across 28 reels the "Vox feel" is restraint, not abundance [M]:

| Dimension | Norm | Where measured |
|---|---|---|
| Elements on screen | ≤ 3 (hero, caption, one annotation) | @chrismoran__ 12/12 |
| Accent colours per film | 1 (chrismoran) to 2 (earn.edits) | both |
| Accent marks per frame | 1 (one red arrow OR one circle) | chrismoran hooks 12/12 |
| Motion moves per scene | ≤ 2, everything else holds | chrismoran |
| Hero hold when it is the point | ≥ 5 s, up to 17.8 s with slow drift | earn.edits d08, eg9, yur |
| Shot change | every 2–4 s; < 2 s only inside a spoken list | all three studies |

Hierarchy is two tiers, never merged: the **hero word** (1–3 words, huge, cropped by the frame
edge, one accent, held 3–8 s) and the **caption** (2–4 words, small, same box, word-synced) [M].
Sendaydiego's warning: a polished, perfect look makes the piece "resemble an ad rather than
editorial content" [S]. Perfectionism is the wrong target; legibility and provenance are.

## 5. The narrator

- Voice is the spine: present from frame 0 to the last word in 24 of 24 measured reels; no
  silence > 0.4 s [M].
- Written to be spoken: "you can't really afford to be too clever or beautiful when you're doing
  voice-over"; phrases like "Look at this" and "That's right" are not filler, they give the viewer
  time to absorb [S Fong, Open Notebook].
- Vox's founding rule: no desks, no anchor; and the host, when present, must be "there for a
  reason, as opposed to just a crutch" [S Posner/Nieman; Fong].
- Measured role of a host in short form: 2–4 talking-head cutaways of 3–4 s as a pace reset, never
  as the backbone [M @chrismoran__]; Zhylin returns to camera three times (0, 34, 58 s) to re-anchor
  [M].
- Calm, loud, consistent: speech mean about -20 dBFS RMS with no spikes above it [M].

## 6. What amateurs get wrong

| Amateur habit | Why it fails the contract | What the pros do |
|---|---|---|
| Frantic cutting on every beat | The voice did not ask for a change; the viewer loses the argument | Change only when the noun changes; hold ≥ 5 s on the hero [M] |
| Decoration instead of evidence | Pretty pieces that do not prove the sentence; a ritual object shown under a line about handwritten notes [M] | Literal object for every noun; the proof is the shot [S Fong] |
| Effects for their own sake | Zooms, particles, glows without new information | Every move promises new information at its end [S editing theory] |
| Music/SFX over the voice | Intelligibility is the mix | Voice-led, music 10–20 dB under, SFX only on cuts [S AES; M] |
| Full sentences on screen | Reader and listener compete | 2–4-word captions + 1–3-word hero [M] |
| Three accents in one frame | No hierarchy; nothing is the point | One mark, one colour [M] |
| Whip into empty frames | Dead frames break the spell | Every frame has a subject [M earn.edits] |
| Proof shown 1 s, covered by a caption | Viewer cannot read the evidence | Hold ≥ 2.5 s, caption never over the proof [M] |
| Outro that drops 8 elements at once | Rushed ending kills recall | The last spoken line is the CTA; ≤ 3 new elements in the first second [M] |
| Sanitising a grim story because the look is pretty | Betrays the journalism | "Not just because it's beautiful or pretty" [S Sendaydiego] |

## 7. Why the restraint wins (the mechanism) [I, grounded in S/M]

Each beat is a mini-proof: *claim (voice) → object (screen) → mark (accent) → hold (time to read)*.
Restraint keeps each of the four legible; frenzy collapses them into noise. The slow push-in on a
document is the camera saying "this is real, look closer". Texture says "this came from somewhere".
The lone red arrow says "here, exactly". The 5-second hold says "I trust you to read it". Together
they produce the sensation of being shown, not told, which is the only thing the viewer cannot get
from a text article.
