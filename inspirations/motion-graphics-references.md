# Motion graphics references (UI / product motion, kinetic type, launch films)

Studied 2026-10-09. Links and analysis only: nothing of these works is stored here. Local frames
live in the gitignored `inspirations/frames/` and are never published.

Grammar only, never identity: take pacing, camera, type behaviour and structure; never a
product's UI, logo, palette or copy. Each entry maps to a pattern of the `motion-graphics` skill:
**product reel**, **UI morph loop**, **kinetic statement**, **layer by layer**.

How each entry was studied: **W** = watched, frames at 1–2 fps plus timings read off the sheet;
**R** = read from the case study or docs only (no frame check, so its claims are the source's,
not mine).

## A. Real UI on a stage (product reel)

| # | Piece | Link | Study |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Linear, "Introducing Linear Agent" (55 s, 2:1) | <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRql2VJ99gM> | W |
| 2 | Raycast, "New Raycast. Coming 2026" (39 s) | <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mi173xGb0ZA> | W |
| 3 | Framer, "That's Framer" (38 s) | <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8AHPXm9Y6mI> | W |
| 4 | Notion, "Introducing the new Notion AI" (57 s) | <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S92KX8-Hmlc> | W |

**1. Linear Agent.** Maps to *layer by layer* + product reel.
- The UI is the only actor: black frame, interface tilted in oblique perspective with a heavy
  vignette and shallow depth, so only the part being read is sharp. No device, no hand.
- One slow dolly across the surface for ~40 s; nothing is labelled. The prompt is typed into the
  field, the result list fills in, and the camera slides to the next level
  (list → detail → doc) instead of cutting.
- Ending: the wordmark resolves from blur to sharp (~1 s), the tagline types in beside it, then
  a still mark holds ~4 s. Quiet, confident, no CTA text.
- Take: perspective-tilted UI + slow travel makes a real recording feel cinematic; depth is the
  only emphasis device; end on the wordmark resolving, not on a card.

**2. Raycast.** Maps to *UI morph loop* + product reel.
- The product is shown as glass pills floating on a dark sculpted-silk ground (black/white
  folds). A query is typed ("cl" → "clipboard"), the pill's result list expands in place.
- The emoji grid is the best beat (≈14–16 s): a highlighted frame hops from cell to cell, so one
  gesture reads as one idea. A dictation pill (waveform) morphs
  from the same capsule.
- The tail is a run of tiny centred text cards on black, one per second: `NEW / COMPACT MODE`,
  `NEW / VOICE FEATURES`, `NEW / AI CHAT` …, then `NEW RAYCAST / COMING 2026` and the mark.
  Small type held in a lot of black reads premium, and the repeated `NEW /` prefix makes a list
  with no bullets.
- Take: one capsule that grows and shrinks; a hopping focus ring; feature list as a metronome of
  micro-cards.

**3. Framer.** Maps to product reel.
- Opens on a glossy 3D ribbon (the brand object) with a tiny pill UI floating in it, then the
  headline "The Internet Canvas" lands as a big stacked type block. The brand object comes
  back between sections as a visual rest.
- Feature beats are the real editor on black with a cursor; finished sites fan out as a stack
  of cards flying through z (≈16–18 s). The film ends by clicking `Publish` (cursor press, button
  state) before the mark.
- Take: one 3D brand object as the recurring "rest"; a cursor click as the literal resolution
  (publish = the end of the story).

**4. Notion AI.** Maps to *kinetic statement* alternating with product reel (the cleanest
structure of the set).
- Strict A/B rhythm: a centred statement on off-white (2–3 s: "Find answers from all your
  apps", "Write smarter, with AI that knows your style") → the same idea shown as the real UI
  in a card on a saturated flat ground (blue, orange-red, yellow) with a cursor, 4–8 s.
- Each feature owns one colour; the statement is always the same size and place, so only the
  ground and content change. A round icon-in-circle appears in the corner as a recurring tag.
- Take: statement → proof alternation; one colour per feature; a fixed statement slot that
  never moves.

## B. Concept films and type-led launch

| # | Piece | Link | Study |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | The Browser Company, "Arc 1.0: Everything or nothing" (34 s) | <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n5Vwrj1gEWs> | W |
| 6 | Vercel Ship 2024 save-the-date film (36 s, 60 fps) | <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PHvw0IP7dEU> | W |
| 7 | Figma Config 2024 identity and opening film (Figma + Relay) | <https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/figma-config-2024-identity-graphic-design-spotlight-090724> | R |

**5. Arc 1.0.** Maps to *kinetic statement*.
- The script is a pun on the version number: serif words ("version", "everything", "can mean",
  "nothing") cut full-frame on black, each held ~1 s, while framed archival clips of famous
  products and failed inventions flash between them one per second, each with a tiny caption
  line under the frame.
- Black-and-white archival footage (a ropeway and a glider) opens and closes the film (≈0–4 s
  and ≈21–24 s): a bookend that is also the metaphor.
- The product appears only after the concept lands (≈25 s: `v1.0` in the same serif, then UI on
  a cream ground), and the CTA is one saturated blue field with the mark and URL.
- Take: concept first, product last; one serif voice for the whole statement; archival
  collage as rhythm (one image = one beat); a single saturated colour reserved for the CTA.

**6. Vercel Ship 2024.** Maps to *kinetic statement* (a title-sequence register).
- A macro camera slides over giant 3D letterforms ("SHIP" sliced into segments that assemble),
  across speaker portrait cards, a "Get Tickets" label, and a flip-clock countdown. The camera is
  never still and never shows the whole object.
- Particle dissolve is the transition: edges of type and labels disperse into grain (≈6–8 s,
  ≈13–14 s). The logo triangle emerges from noise in the first 2 s.
- The end is tiny mono type on black (`23 MAY 2024 / NEW YORK CITY`, then the URL), each card
  faded in and out; the wordmark exits with a horizontal squash.
- Take: macro camera on physical-feeling type; grain/particle dissolve as a branded transition;
  tiny monospaced info cards as the quiet end.

**7. Figma Config 2024.** Maps to *UI morph loop* thinking applied to an identity (read only).
- Source: motion extends the identity's shape language; shapes touch and "activate" one another
  and fill the full screen height over a ~1 min open film; glyphs of the wordmark transform;
  six palettes on different grounds keep the motion from feeling repeated; the film was paced for
  the 9 am slot where the audience counts it down.
- Take: one shape vocabulary reused for every transition; palette variants instead of new
  motion; pace the opener for the room it plays in.

- **8. Canva / Affinity.** Per the feature: 3D, 2D motion, kinetic type and hand-drawn
  illustration in one fast "journey through time, space and the tool's flow", with the product
  demo (a selection outline and cursor choosing Vector / Pixel / Layout) inside the collage.
  Take: a product demo can sit inside an illustrated world if the cursor and selection outline
  stay legible. Pattern: product reel with a collage ground.
- **9. Power to the Pro.** Per the entry: a base algorithmic structure drives every form and
  movement, "supercharged" into a procedural megacity, built on the product being sold.
  Take: one procedural rule generating all motion gives coherence; the film is evidence of the
  product's power. Pattern: kinetic statement / showpiece.
- **10. Ray-Ban Stories.** Per the case study: a bold motion language built on the brand red and
  adapted to 16:9, 9:16 and a centre-cut format; a tiny-file AR filter whose type was
  extruded into concentric rings. Take: design the motion system once, then reflow it per
  aspect ratio (centre-cut safe area), not re-animate. Pattern: product reel system.
- **11. Wonderful tools.** Per the write-up: one animated world morphs from product to product
  across ~2 min, so each device grows out of the previous one instead of cutting; the last
  caption states the thesis. Take: match-cut by transformation, thesis line last.
  Pattern: layer by layer / morph.

## D. Easing, springs and the motion that feels expensive

| # | Piece | Link | Study |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | Apple WWDC23, "Animate with springs" | <https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10158/> | R |
| 13 | Steve Lemuel, Raycast spec teaser (Contra case study, not by Raycast) | <https://contra.com/p/llNMeeMX-kinetic-product-motion-for-raycast-making-a-ui-feel-alive> | R |

- **12. Springs.** The doc settles the engine's spring vocabulary: pick *duration* first (pace),
  then *bounce* (character); default bounce 0; ≈0.15 is barely bouncy, ≈0.30 clearly bouncy,
  above ≈0.4 too much for UI. A spring retargeted mid-flight inherits its current velocity, so
  interruptions never jerk. Linear motion jumps in velocity at start and end, so avoid it for
  movement. Take: spec every ease as duration + bounce; retarget, never restart.
- **13. Spec teaser.** A 15 s social-first piece: UI rebuilt in Figma and animated with fast,
  high-frequency easing, then tracked into 4K footage so it sits in a physical workspace; bold
  repeating kinetic type; one mechanical audio cue per shortcut; a teal/orange grade. Treat as
  a technique list, not a quality bar (a freelancer's spec work). Take: UI tracked into a real
  scene; one sound per action.

## Patterns to steal

1. **Statement → proof alternation** (Notion): fixed-size statement card, then the real UI on one
   colour per feature. Keeps type and product in separate registers.
2. **Tilt and travel the real recording** (Linear): oblique perspective, vignette, one slow
   dolly; depth as the only emphasis.
3. **One capsule that grows and shrinks** (Raycast): one container for search, grid, dictation;
   a focus ring that hops cells.
4. **Micro-card list** (Raycast): `NEW / THING` cards at ~1 s, tiny type, lots of black.
5. **Concept first, product last** (Arc): a one-line pun carried in one serif, archival frames
   one per beat, a single saturated colour reserved for the CTA.
6. **Macro camera and grain dissolve** (Vercel): never show the whole object; particles as the
   branded transition; small mono info cards to end.
7. **Resolve the wordmark** (Linear): blur to sharp over ~1 s, tagline types in, then a long
   still hold.
8. **Cursor click as the ending** (Framer): the story ends on the literal action (Publish).
9. **Spring spec = duration + bounce, retarget not restart** (Apple).
10. **Design the system once, reflow per ratio** (Buck, Figma): one shape vocabulary, palette
    variants, centre-cut safe area.
