## Structure (line of reasoning)
Promise "5 tricks from Vox" (card 1.5 s) → five numbered chapters, each opened by the SAME stamp card:
grey paper with a faint engraved portrait, a big black serif numeral, a small yellow Vox dot top-left, then the
trick name builds under it in serif italics (one word per beat). Each chapter: card (1.5-2.5 s) → proof clips of Vox
doing it (examples) → one sentence of "why". Last chapter pays off: "it's not only about graphics, it's how they put
everything together" (storytelling + editing + graphics = "VOX FORMULA ? ? ?") → CTA.
Viewer learns: 1 visualize everything, 2 use motion design to explain ideas (maps, newspapers, diagrams, cutout photos),
3 constant motion (nothing stays more than a few seconds), 4 seamless transitions (match cuts, zooms), 5 the formula is the mix.

## General takeaways
1. Captions burned in, 2-4 words, always the same spot: the viewer reads along; the number card makes chapters countable.
2. One reusable stamp card per chapter (numeral + yellow dot + same paper) = rhythm and orientation; reusable component.
3. Cut rate is high (1-1.7 s) but every cut shows ONE subject for ONE noun: nothing is decorative.
4. Fast montage only under a list sentence; explanatory sentences get a 3-5 s hold (the Trump/bill section).
5. Voice is constant and loud (-14 LUFS); SFX are rare (~1 per 6 s), only on card/transition, and never mask the voice.
6. The host returns three times (0, 34, 58) to re-anchor; 5 s outro is plain and calm, a spoken CTA without a designed lockup.
7. Reasoning: promise N, deliver N, last item reframes the list ("not only X, it's the mix").
