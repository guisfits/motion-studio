# Structure reference: hooks, beats, rate, beat sheet

Legend: **[S]** sourced · **[M]** measured in the 28-reel reference studies (`inspirations/`) · **[I]** inference.

## 1. The shape

```
HOOK (0–4.5 s)  →  WHY / CONTEXT  →  TURN (40–60 %)  →  PAYOFF  →  CLOSE (calm beat)  →  CTA (≤ 4.5 s)
```

- **Hook**: one complete sentence, said whole, made of a fact, a number, a "What if", or a
  command to look [M earn.edits 5/5 at 1.9–4.7 s; @chrismoran__ 6/12 "Look at this / See it?",
  6/12 paradox or promise]. Subject on frame 0, voice at 0.0 s [M 24/24].
- **Why / context**: who, when, how; each sentence gets its object.
- **Turn**: one explicit pivot word at roughly half the runtime: "But it gets crazier", "Why?",
  "And here's a strange fact", "Remove it and it loses that feel" [M earn.edits 5/5; chrismoran 11/12
  have a "take it away / before-after" beat].
- **Payoff**: the answer to the opening question, or the reframe ("it's not only about graphics,
  it's how they put everything together") [M Zhylin].
- **Close**: a calm sentence or question alone on a plain ground for ~2.5 s before any pivot
  [M earn.edits eg9, d08].
- **CTA**: one spoken line ≤ 11 words, ≤ 4.5 s, on a real visual of what is offered (or on the
  narrator's face), ends on the address or the face; whole outro 4.9–5.8 s = 12–18 % of the film [M earn.edits]; or the CTA is simply the last
  spoken line then a hard cut, no card [M chrismoran 12/12].

Vox long-form has the same bones at scale: question → why it is hard → evidence → reveal. Fong
opened the black-hole film with the telescopes so viewers understood the difficulty before seeing
the fuzzy image, and delayed the reveal on purpose [S Open Notebook].

## 2. Hook types (with the image under them)

| Type | Example | Image under it | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shock number | "Warren Buffett has $381 billion in cash." | counter ticking, red rings | [M yur] |
| Odd fact | "Fake cocaine sent him to the hospital, and the FBI seized the movie's profits." | hospital gurney, actor with a dollar | [M eg9] |
| "What if" reframe | "What if aging was never the problem, but the fear of it was?" | face, floating words | [M eb2] |
| Command to look | "Look at this Apollo 11 photo. Look closer. Notice that tiny movement?" | the photo, one red arrow | [M Da3YpRipHuF] |
| Paradox | "This texture feels tactile, this one feels off... same texture." | side by side | [M DcBhNhOg5kR] |
| Promise N | "Five tricks Vox uses..." / "Three editing effects Vox uses all the time" | numbered card | [M Zhylin, DaNlGAcgYsD] |
| Border-style question | "Why is the border straight here?" beats "the history of Africa" | the map, the line | [S Korpi, third-party] |

Rule: the hook image is literal and iconic, never abstract [M]. The hook object stays the subject
for the whole first beat (0–5 s) with at most one annotation [M chrismoran 12/12].

## 3. Beats

A beat = one sentence of 2.5–4.5 s + one visual idea [M earn.edits]. Third-party Vox pipelines
define it as "1–3 sentences, 8–14 s spoken + one visual" for long-form [S, third-party]. In short
form, keep one beat per ground; the ground change is the idea change [M].

Write the script as a **three-column script**: ears / eyes / sources (Abram) [S]; or the classic
two-column audio/visual [S LearningGuild]. Every line of voice must have its object in the eyes
column before recording; a line with no object is cut or rewritten (Abram, Caswell) [S].

## 4. Word rate and length

| Quantity | Norm | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Speaking rate | 2.25–3.0 words/s (135–180 wpm), median 2.5 ≈ 150 wpm | [M earn.edits 12/12; chrismoran]; vendor consensus 140–160 wpm, 110–130 for dense technical, never ~200 [S] |
| Words per 30 s film | 70–90 | arithmetic from above |
| Words per 45 s film | 100–135 | [M earn.edits 80–106 words at 27–47 s] |
| Words per 60 s film | 135–180 | [S vendors: a 60 s explainer ≈ 140–150 words] |
| Reel length (argument, not pitch) | 27–50 s; median 36 (earn.edits) / 43 (chrismoran) | [M]; a 15–25 s film "has no room to reason" [M] |
| Vox medium-form | 5–15 min; long investigations ~30 min; average watch time ≈ 4 min is the retention target | [S Press Gazette; Storybench] |
| Howtown feature | 15–20 min, 6 weeks, half of it research | [S Storybench] |

Vertical-length evidence, honestly weighed [S]: marketing blogs claim 7–15 s Reels retain 60–80 %
and 15–30 s retain 40–60 %, but none shows data; one analysis notes a 45 s Reel at 70 % retention
beats a 15 s Reel at 90 % on total seconds watched; vendors report top Shorts cluster at 25–35 s
and the average Short runs ~33 s. YouTube's own Shorts analytics separate "swiped away" from
"stayed", so early seconds carry the most weight (roughly 3 % of watch time per second in a 30 s
Short, one creator's arithmetic). **Default [I from M+S]: a reasoning Reel runs 45–60 s (90 max) — the
references cluster at 36–43 s, but a reel with context, a turn and a payoff needs the extra room;
a single-point clip 20–30 s; never below 27 s if it has a turn.**

## 5. Vox writing habits

- **Video-first scripts.** Producers write their own scripts; writers were brought in to narrate
  at most. Video "requires a different kind of thinking" (Fong) [S The Long Story].
- **Clean slate.** Fong writes as a non-specialist who assumes the viewer knows nothing she did
  not know a week ago; script editors are "so important for them to be like, 'I was confused at this
  part'" [S Open Notebook].
- **Viewer cannot glance back.** "There's a really high risk of charging forward and not realizing
  that the viewer is not with you." Keep "That's right", "Look at this" as absorption pauses [S].
- **Plain speech.** No clever or ornate language in VO; explain it to a friend, start with "so"
  (JEA, secondhand) [S].
- **Research until it repeats.** Edwards stops when new sources repeat facts he already has;
  Caswell built an original dataset before pitching [S].
- **Pitch as headline + thumbnail.** Caswell pitches 3 ideas with headline + synopsis; Edwards
  greenlights only a "really clicky" thumbnail + headline [S].
- **Music changes mark structure.** Fong: "every 20 seconds I'm stopping and changing a song";
  starts and endings are attention cues [S]. In short form the equivalent is the ground change [M].
- **Timeline.** Caswell: script 4–5 days, animation + VO + assembly 7–10 days, ~3 weeks total;
  Vox output 1–2 videos/week, 2–3 weeks each; Edwards 2-week cycle, week 1 research + script [S].
