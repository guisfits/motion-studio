# Scroll-stopping video ads

## Overview

In a feed nobody came to buy. The viewer scrolls on autopilot (Kahneman's System 1), so the first
seconds of an ad do not sell the product: they **buy the next ten seconds of attention**. The hook
carries most of the result (practitioners say ~80%), because nobody sees the offer if they leave
at second 5. And the first sentence is the real targeting: it pulls the qualified viewer and repels
the merely curious, and platform AIs read it to choose who sees the ad.

Source: Bia Feldman, "Faça seus criativos assim" (YouTube, nrHeOhCazqU), a direct-response
framework, kept here with an honesty filter (see Ethics).

## The deliverable (what the script IS)

Produce these parts, in this order:

1. **Hook bank** — 3–6 hooks, at least one per type below, each tagged with its type and the
   proven pattern it was mined from.
2. **Pain ladder** — the five whys for the target viewer, level 0 to 5, then the one everyday
   **micro-scene** the film will show.
3. **Script in four blocks** with timestamps (table below), one line of picture per beat.
4. **CTA** written as curiosity or value, plus the on-screen text.
5. **Ethics check** — every claim true and sourced; list what you refused.

## 1 · Mine hooks, don't invent them

Take patterns humans already proved they click:
- YouTube: search the niche, sort by views; isolate the **image + promise** pair of top thumbnails/titles.
- Podcast clips: counterintuitive statements, clashing opinions, an expert saying the unexpected.
- Ad libraries (Meta, TikTok Creative Center): ads running for months are validated.

Adapt the pattern to your true subject; never copy the words.

**Three hook types:**

| Type | Mechanism | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Counterintuitive | breaks common sense; the brain must resolve the contradiction | "The innocent habit of X may be why Y" |
| Open loop | Zeigarnik: the mind hates unfinished stories; answer comes later | a question or claim paid off at ≈ 30–40 s |
| Identity filter | calls the viewer **by behaviour, not label** | "If you've read the same verse three times…" not "Christians:" |

Specific beats generic: a concrete object, number, time or habit ("3 a.m.", "a glass of water on
the nightstand") stops the scroll; a category ("anxiety", "poor sleep") does not.

## 2 · Sell the felt pain, not the logical benefit

People decide by emotion and justify with logic. Run the **five whys** from the surface desire
(level 0) to the identity pain (level 5): physical → social behaviour → judgment of others →
internal guilt ("I tried ten times, the problem is me") → identity ("I lost who I was"). Then
write the **micro-frustration** the viewer never says aloud as one everyday scene.

**Indirect projection:** the brain shuts down when attacked ("you are X"). Show a third person or
an everyday situation and let the viewer recognise themselves.

## 3 · The four blocks

| Block | Time | Job | Weak → strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook | 0–5 s (≤ 10) | break the trance, create tension that needs resolving | "Do you suffer from X? Watch to the end." → a specific counterintuitive claim |
| 2 Connection | 5–25 s | show you understand their hell better than they say it; qualify; **open a loop** ("in the next 40 s I'll show why…") | "I know how hard X is" → the micro-scene, in their words |
| 3 Mechanism + future pace | 25 s – CTA | **absorb the guilt** (the old approach failed, not them); name the new mechanism; let them taste the result before paying | "Our method helps you" → why the old ways only treated symptoms, then a concrete picture of life after |
| 4 CTA | last 5–10 s | avoid the sales alarm: the click is an act of intelligence, curiosity or value | "Click and buy now" → "I left the step-by-step here; start with the free part" |

**Video mechanics:** the hook is readable on screen from frame 1 (most viewers start muted) and the
frame moves within 0.5 s. One idea per beat. Make 2–3 hook variants over the same body for A/B.

## Ethics (non-negotiable filter)

The source framework also uses fake urgency ("before they take it down"), conspiracy framing and
gender-ego stereotypes. Do not. Keep the mechanism, drop the lie:

| Tactic | Allowed form |
|---|---|
| Urgency / scarcity | only if true (a real deadline, a real launch price) |
| Social proof | only real, attributable testimonials or numbers; never invented "users say…" |
| Pain | named with dignity; never shame, fear-mongering or health/financial promises |
| "Restricted knowledge" | real curiosity (a fact they didn't know), never a fake secret |
| Brand tone | wins: a sober or religious brand uses curiosity and identity, not fear |

Run `marketing-psychology:dark-pattern-check` when available.

## Common mistakes

- Opening on the product name, a logo or a slow title card.
- Hook as a category question ("Struggling with X?").
- Problem beat that lists contexts ("in traffic, at work, at night") instead of one scene.
- No open loop, so nothing holds the viewer from 10 s to the demo.
- Features as logical benefits; no "it wasn't your fault".
- CTA that shouts "download now"; fabricated testimonials.
